Virtual

We built a phone-based prototype for finding everyday objects. It combines a camera with an arrow, spatial audio and vibration, so guidance doesn’t depend on a screen alone.

01 / OUR VISION

Finding things
without relying only on sight.

Be My Eyes inspired the project. The idea was to explore one specific everyday task: finding an object nearby, such as a cup on a table or an item on a shelf.

A phone seemed like a useful place to start. We could work with its camera and vibration motor, add spatial audio, and let someone choose what they were looking for.

03 / THE APP INTERFACE

Choose a target.
Follow the arrow.

We chose YOLOv3-tiny after researching models suitable for a phone. We initially tried TensorFlowSharp, then moved to Unity Barracuda because its documentation and support made integration easier to work through.

You can choose a detected object or a saved reference image. In image mode, the app tracks that image—a sticker on a notebook, for example. The arrow shows which way to turn the phone toward the selected target.

December 5, 2022ALPHA MILESTONE

Bringing the two modes together.

After our alpha submission, peers tried both modes on December 7. The difference between them was confusing, even with audio and vibration working. That feedback pushed us toward a clearer interface, smoother mode switching and better website instructions.

December 8, 2022FINAL COURSE SUBMISSION

Showing the app and explaining why.

We submitted the final project and brought the app, website and trailer to the class showcase. The website explained the project’s purpose and showed how it worked. We focused on the core interaction and left the unfinished radar for later.
